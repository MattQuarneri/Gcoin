Reimagining the Economy:

A Blockchain based Relative Value Accounting Financial Network for a Social Credit allocation and Gratitude Payment Attribution

Abstract

This paper proposes a transformative economic model built on one insight and three innovations:

  1. We identify and forecast the deflationary power of freely available technical knowledge and its negative impact on absolute value based cost amortization accounting schemes.
  2. We propose recasting financial value accounting from absolute to relative valuations to preserve relative balances and counteract deflationary forces with relative allocations.
  3. We functionally define a free credit, earned gratitude, and invested interest based value accounting framework.
  4. We functionally design a blockchain based financial accounting network for tracking Free Credit, Earned Gratitude, and Invested Interest.

First, we introduce the concept of the negative Free Technology Interest Rate (FTIR) which reflects the increasing power of technical knowledge as a productive force in the economy. We propose that this force will increase to the point where, due to their absolute versus relative value based grounding, traditional fiat currency based financial agreements will be either mathematically insolvent or functionally debased in their current form by 2100.

To counter these forces we propose a peer-to-peer gratitude based relative value accounting financial graph framework that incorporates a daily use-it-or-lose-it Universal Basic Income (UBI) credit flow scheme that makes hoarding impossible and separates long-term investment from daily usage. The framework formalizes an “earned gratitude" value accounting methodology based on relative rather than absolute amounting. Through blockchain technology gratitude is attributable to identifiable network participants and transfers are immutable. To ensure value flows through the network a few rebalancing mechanisms are built into the system to prevent excessive accumulation and flow stagnation.

Second, we outline a blockchain based financial graph network that uses a free credit and earned gratitude scheme to recast traditionally opaque absolute debt into translucent relative partnerships which by its nature of facilitating bi-directional attribution and traceability can foster greater awareness and appreciation of interdependency by economic network participants.

Third, we propose This paper presents both the theoretical foundations and an actionable adoption plan designed to foster a more productive, inclusive, technologically resilient, and cognitively advanced economic ecosystem that enables appreciation and encourages social cohesion through bi-directional attribution and interdependence awareness through relative value quantification.

The "Free Technology" Interest Rate and its Deflationary Impact

2.1 Exponential Technological Acceleration

Due to its ability to recursively impact and improve its own performance, technological progress is exponential, not linear. The breakthroughs and insights of today produce functional knowledge and know-how that improves the productivity of the work of tomorrow thereby reducing the marginal cost of goods and services. The economic value of absolute cost investments diminish while the inverse; the relative value of the relative investment increases. We propose modeling this dynamic as a negative "free technology" interest rate—one that devalues the absolute value debt. Today we amortize the cost of knowledge acquisition over the number of units that knowledge produces and simply factor that into the marginal cost of the unit. But that amortization scheme breaks down when technology progresses faster than the amortization schedule allows forcing a write-off of the unamortized cost. If the acceleration progresses too fast then conventional debt burdens become untenable.  Mulit-billion dollar data center investments fail to perform if technological advancement converts hardware investments into uncapitalizable overcapacity.

This deflationary power of technology paves the way for a new financial paradigm.

2.2 Inversion of the Time Value of Money

In traditional finance, money today is more valuable than money tomorrow. However, if investments in productivity today yield exponentially greater benefits in the future, the time value of money is fundamentally inverted. Under our framework, early investments reduce the effective debt over time, aligning capital allocation with long-term societal benefit rather than short-term profit extraction.

3. Tokenized Investment and Smart Contract Partnerships

3.1 Transforming Capital Allocation

We propose the use of blockchain-based tokenization to convert conventional debt instruments into dynamic, digitally managed partnerships. In this system, tokens represent both a financial claim and a stake in assets that deliver social goods. Smart contracts—automatically applying the negative free technology interest rate—ensure that as technological advances reduce production costs, the associated debt burden declines in real time, independent of traditional repayment schedules.

3.2 From Passive Lending to Active Partnership

By linking financial returns directly to social outcomes, this approach transforms financiers into active partners. Gratitude payments become a central feature: beneficiaries of social goods (such as affordable housing, sustainable transport, or renewable energy) can issue gratitude payments to those whose capital enabled these advances. Over time, these payments accumulate as blockchain-based social credits, setting the stage for a new store of value.

4. Blockchain as the Global Immutable Ledger

4.1 Securing the New Economy

Blockchain technology provides the immutable, global ledger needed for this new system. Every social credit is bound to an individual’s identity through robust verification measures—linking email, phone number, legal name, birth date, and hometown—with additional checks ensuring physical and mental quality. This secure binding ensures that credits cannot be fraudulently transferred or stolen, thereby safeguarding the integrity of the system.

4.2 Transparency and Decentralized Governance

The use of smart contracts guarantees transparency and consistency. Regulatory sandboxes and decentralized governance protocols will be essential to oversee these automated adjustments, ensuring that no single authority can dominate the issuance or distribution of credits. This decentralized approach enhances trust and equity within the network.

5. The Gratitude Framework: Allocation Schemes for Social Credits

5.1 Designing Fair Distribution

To facilitate rational distribution of social credits, we propose several allocation schemes:

  • Fixed Proportional Allocation: A predetermined split (e.g., 60% for fixed costs such as shelter, food, and transport; 40% for discretionary spending) can ensure essential needs are met.
  • User-Defined Allocation: Allowing individuals to set personalized distribution ratios, supported by baseline guidelines and minimum thresholds.
  • Dynamic Adaptive Allocation: Leveraging data analytics and machine-learning to adjust allocations based on consumption patterns and economic indicators.
  • Peer Voting and Reputation-Based Allocation: Incorporating community feedback and reputation scores to shift allocations toward underfunded essential services.
  • Market-Based Mechanism: Allowing service providers to set “prices” in credits, enabling market dynamics to dictate the relative scarcity and value of services.
  • Hybrid Mechanism: Combining fixed, adaptive, and market-based elements to balance stability with responsiveness.

These schemes aim to ensure that credits are distributed fairly and reflect both individual needs and broader societal priorities.

6. Universal Basic Credits and Rebalancing Mechanisms

6.1 Daily UBI Allocation and Graditude Payments

In our model, every network participant receives a fixed daily allocation of credits —set, for instance, at 1000 credits or perhaps one credit per minute —as a form of Universal Basic Income (UBI). These credits provide a guaranteed baseline for meeting essential expenses and must be fully spent each day with unspent credits disappearing to disincentivize hoarding, and ensure  active circulation within the economy.

6.2 Accumulation of Earned Credits

Daily “G-coins” will be allotted by the coin holder to cover basic living expenses with extra available for their discretionary usage. Allocation of coinage will be based largely on current allocations of income. So if an individual spends 40% of their general income on their mortgage, that allocation will persist. This proportional allocation methodology also allows for a pathway to transition from earned credits to UBI credits.

In addition to the daily UBI, individuals and institutions can accumulate additional credits through gratitude payments. Unlike UBI credits, these earned credits can be saved, representing a store of social value that rewards those who contribute to communal well-being.

6.3 Rebalancing Mechanisms

To prevent disproportionate accumulation and maintain equity across the network, we propose several rebalancing strategies:

  • Credit Decay Function: Implementing a gradual depreciation on accumulated credits to discourage hoarding.
  • Progressive Accumulation Tax: Introducing a modest, progressive tax on credit balances that exceed certain thresholds, with collected credits redistributed or reinvested in public goods.
  • Dynamic Redistribution Protocols: Utilizing smart contracts to trigger periodic rebalancing events, reallocating excess credits to underfunded areas or directly to UBI accounts.
  • Diminishing Marginal Utility Adjustments: Adjusting the conversion rate or purchasing power of credits beyond a certain level to incentivize reinvestment over accumulation.

These measures ensure that the system remains balanced and that the social credit economy continues to reflect equitable resource distribution.

7. Adoption Plan and Implementation Strategy

7.1 Pilot Projects and Regulatory Frameworks

  • Phase 1 – Concept Validation:
    Launch pilot projects in targeted sectors (e.g., renewable energy, affordable housing) to test the application of negative free technology interest rates and tokenized investments. Engage regulatory bodies to create a controlled sandbox environment.
  • Phase 2 – Smart Contract Deployment:
    Develop and deploy audited smart contracts that apply the free technology interest rate and integrate with identity verification systems. Ensure these contracts can handle both UBI distribution and gratitude payments.
  • Phase 3 – Scaling and Integration:
    Expand the pilot projects across multiple sectors and regions. Collaborate with academic institutions, industry leaders, and policymakers to refine governance models and achieve interoperability with existing financial systems.

7.2 Key Performance Metrics

  • Technological Progress Index:
    Develop quantitative metrics to track the rate of technological advancements, serving as the calibration basis for the free technology interest rate.
  • Social Impact Metrics:
    Monitor improvements in essential services—housing affordability, energy efficiency, public health—to gauge the social benefits of tokenized investments.
  • Security and Identity Assurance:
    Regular audits of the identity-binding mechanisms and blockchain integrity to ensure the system’s security and prevent fraudulent activity.

8. Policy Implications and Broader Economic Impact

The proposed framework has the potential to reshape traditional debt structures and capital allocation methods. By aligning financial incentives with technological progress and social welfare, it could:

  • Rebalance Debt:
    Convert traditional debt obligations into dynamically decreasing liabilities, thereby reducing long-term financial burdens.
  • Foster Equitable Capital Allocation:
    Shift investment from short-term profit-driven models to socially beneficial projects.
  • Encourage Sustainable Innovation:
    Create an environment where investments in public goods and infrastructure yield both financial and societal dividends.
  • Strengthen Social Cohesion:
    Transform financial transactions into opportunities for reciprocal recognition and community building through gratitude payments and social credits.

9. Conclusion

This paper has presented a visionary framework that integrates negative free technology interest rates, tokenized investment mechanisms, and a blockchain-based Gratitude Framework to forge a new economic paradigm. By coupling a daily UBI with dynamic, earned social credits—and by instituting robust rebalancing mechanisms—the proposed system redefines the role of debt, capital, and social exchange. As technological progress continues to accelerate, this model envisions a future where traditional money is supplanted by a digital credit system anchored in communal well-being and sustained by the immutable record of blockchain technology.

We invite scholars, policymakers, and industry practitioners to collaborate on refining and piloting this framework. Through empirical research and iterative experimentation, we can work toward an economy that is more inclusive, resilient, and aligned with the transformative power of technological progress.
