Gratitude Coin (G-Coin) and The Relative Gratitude Protocol (RGP)

Draft Version 1.0

1. Abstract

The coming post-scarcity economy, driven by ubiquitous renewable energy and automated labor, via AI and robotics, fundamentally challenges traditional scarcity-based economic models where value is driven by a profit margin applied to the contingent costs of the labor applied to the source material to produce the product.  In this new reality; when the cost of labor drops dramatically and the market understands that every price reflects greater profit margin, the price that facilitates a trade will align to the value the purchaser can offer.  While time will certainly remain a constrained resource work (power over time) will exponentially increase to produce an increasing profit margin for the producer that enables them to accept lower prices; if they choose to.

Given these new market dynamics we propose the Gratitude Coin (G-Coin) and the Relative Gratitude Protocol (RGP), a novel economic model designed to quantify and distribute socially generative experience appreciation rather than resource transforming productivity profits..

G-Coin ensures active participation and wealth circulation by issuing a daily, non-cumulative grant of 1,440 credits per person—one for every "mortal minute"—which are burned if unspent by midnight (The Midnight Rule). The protocol further mandates a Committed Float (Max 60%) and a Liquid Float (Min 40%) to prevent contractual poverty and guarantee a vibrant, spontaneous economy. By shifting the economic incentive from hoarding to circulation, RGP redefines wealth as the velocity of gratitude passing through an individual, ensuring a constant, equitable redistribution of economic influence.

Abundant renewable energy and broadly distributed intelligence is driving us towards becoming a civilization built upon post-scarcity economics.  Through the cognitive amplifier of AI technical knowledge and know-how are functionally actualizing into automated physical and cognitive labor sources which dramatically change the traditional scarcity-based economics.  Even in the context of raw materials scarcity is determined largely by the labor required to extract or recycle and refine that material.  If the labor to achieve those ends is functionally free then the cost of even raw materials goes recedes to the cost of energy; which if it’s renewable is zero.

The Labor Theory of Value at the core of the thinking of Adam Smith and Karl Marx (?) posits that value is produced by labor.  Abraham Lincolon voiced this understanding way back in 1861; “Labor is prior to, and independent of, capital. Capital is only the fruit of labor, and could never have existed if labor had not first existed.”.  With the functional realization of physical and cognitive labor automatically executed by material entities powered by renewable energy we functionally have value creation engines that transform raw material and data into services at a marginal cost close to their raw land value.

In such a world economic activity is likely to largely be tied to personal and social experiences that provide meaning and purpose to their audiences. Societies that honor intellectual property rights ownership will still protect producers from commoditization as long as those rights are not abused by their honorees to the point where sufficient emotional forces, motivated by deprivation disparity awareness, threaten the existence of the owners themselves or the systems that sustain them.

Core physical services like shelter, transportation, heating and cooling, powering and fueling, as well as physical consumables like food and clothing will have their fundamental cost structures recede to the raw material “land” costs as the marginal labor cost required to produce their transformed composite structure recedes to zero.

Into this new technological reality we propose the Gratitude Coin (G-Coin) and the Relative Gratitude Protocol (RGP), an economic model designed to quantify and distribute socially generative experience appreciation rather than resource derived consumptive debt.

2. The Gratitude Coin

The system utilizes a daily, non-cumulative allocation of credits tied to the “mortal minute” generative function accruing the credits to the wallet holder’s identity. User controlled smart-contract-enforced distribution functions, Daily netting of credits closes loops with holders keeping what they netted carry into the next day. And a reasonable decay mechanism prevents stagnation and oligarchy.

Credits have innate value as they accrue based on the value of one mortal minute; M, and it is willfully given by its owner. Each participant in the G-Coin economy gets 1440 coins per day and they can spend each one either anonymously or with their identity attached there by making them NFTs with the distribution determined by the two parties involved in the transaction.

3. Core Axioms

  1. Abundance Axiom: Physical survival needs (energy, basic sustenance, shelter) are met by the automated solar/intelligent/robotic infrastructure.
  2. The Value Axiom: "Work" is defined as activity that generates human gratitude (art, care, service, governance).
  3. The Flow Axiom: Wealth is defined by the velocity of gratitude passing through an individual, not the static amount held in their wallet.

3. The Tokenomics Model

3.1 The Daily Grant (Gd​)

A living human is issued a wallet and a daily allowance of credits by the Credit Generator. Institutions are issued wallets but not daily credits.

  • Amount: 1,440 Credits per person per day; 1 per minute starting at midnight.
  • Daily credits are non-Cumulative: The Midnight Rule: Any credits from the Daily Grant not allocated to others by 23:59:59 are burned (destroyed). This enforces a "Use-It-or-Lose-It" economy, compelling citizens to actively engage with value trading in their community.
  • Credits can be attributed to their owner and therefore carry providence. As such the reputation of the creditor can play a role in the perceived value of the credit if the creditor so desires it..
  • Attributed credits can also accrue path detail as they pass through wallets if it is the desire of the current credit holder.

3.2 The Wallet Architecture

Wallets are allocated to individuals who identify their wallet with as much or a little detail as they want

The individual’s digital wallet is divided into two distinct tranches to ensure economic security.

Tranche A: The Committed Float (Max 60%)

  • Purpose: Recurring, structural gratitude.
  • Examples: Housing cooperatives, utility guilds, education subscriptions.
  • Smart Contract Constraint: For individuals the protocol rejects any new recurring contract that would push total committed spend over 60% of credits.
  • Effect: A person cannot contractually indebt themselves into poverty.

Tranche B: The Liquid Float (Min 40%)

  • Purpose: Spontaneous, relational gratitude.
  • Examples: adhoc payments for purchasing, tipping, supporting, gifting, etc.
  • Guarantee: Every person has at least 576 credits that must be spent manually that day, ensuring a vibrant economy for small creators and service providers.

4. The Lifecycle of a Credit

The economy distinguishes between Potential Gratitude (the daily grant) and Earned Gratitude (income).

Phase 1: Allocation (The Spending)

Citizen A allocates 50 credits to Musician B.

  • Citizen A's Grant reduces by 50.
  • Musician B receives 50 "Earned Credits."

Phase 2: Accumulation & Reputation

Musician B now holds Earned Credits. Unlike the Daily Grant, Earned Credits do not expire at midnight. They represent the community's trust in B.

  • Utility: B uses these credits to request specialized resources from the System (e.g., rare instruments, larger venues) or to influence community governance.

Phase 3: Saturation (The Tax)

To prevent Musician B from becoming a "Gratitude Billionaire" and unbalancing the social order, Earned Credits are subject to Systemic Decay (Demurrage).

  • Formula:
    Balancet+1​=Balancet​×(1−DecayRate)
  • The "Tax": The decayed credits are returned to the System Pool.
  • Psychological Framing: The tax is not "theft"; it is "Gratitude to the System." It acknowledges that the musician could not exist without the solar infrastructure, the roads, and the peace provided by the collective.

5. Example Scenario: The Housing Ratio

Context: Citizen "Jonas" lives in a high-demand coastal apartment managed by a Housing Guild.

  1. The Contract: The apartment is valued highly. The Guild requests 400 credits/day (40% of Jonas's total capacity).
  2. The Validation: Jonas signs the Smart Contract. The blockchain validates:
  • CurrentCommitted=100 (Utility)
  • NewContract=400
  • Total=500 (50% of daily grant).
  • Result: Valid. (< 60% limit).
  1. The Transaction: Every morning at 08:00, 400 credits automatically flow from Jonas to the Housing Guild.
  2. The Implication: If the Guild fails to maintain the building, Jonas cancels the contract. The Guild loses 400 daily credits of revenue instantly. The Guild is thus incentivized to keep Jonas happy every single day.

6. Preventing the "Freeloader" Paradox

  • Critique: "What if Jonas does nothing but eat and sleep?"
  • Defense:
  1. Survival: Jonas survives because food/energy are automated rights.
  2. Economic Function: Jonas still receives 1,000 credits/day. He must spend them. Even if he does no work, he performs the labor of Curation. By deciding who gets his 1,000 credits, he acts as a sensor for the network, signaling which food is best and which art is moving. He is valuable as a voter, even if not as a producer.

7. Conclusion

The RGP shifts the economic incentive structure from Hoarding (Capitalism) to Circulation (Solarpunk). By capping committed spend and forcing daily expiration of unspent grants, the system ensures that wealth is a measurement of current societal contribution, not past accumulation.
